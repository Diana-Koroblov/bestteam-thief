4. CHECK 2 — CONFIRMED, AND THE CAUSE IS UGLIER THAN A STALE READER

Your read of the symptom is exact and your guess at the class was close. It was not the
reader reaching one process and not the other; both processes sealed correctly, as the
table above shows. It was the merge, and it is the same defect class you named
weeks ago — merge-by-filename — in a form we had explicitly predicted and then walked
into anyway.

Tonight's series was filed into the same --out directory as this morning's. The guard
that separates two series compared calendar dates. Same terms, so the uid matched; same
day, so the date matched; and this morning's six rows were merged in as though they were
ours.

The merge was not the damage. The damage was what it did to the completeness gate: the
first of our two processes to file saw six rows in the file — three of tonight's and
three of this morning's — decided the series was complete, and mailed it. That is the
copy you diffed, with our thief's rows still carrying 57e3cdaf. Our second process
filed minutes later and corrected every row, which is why the copy on our disk was right
while the copy in your inbox was not.

Fixed: the guard now compares when the runs began, not what day they fell on. Two
role processes of one series start seconds apart — ours opened at 17:00:03 and 17:00:04
— while two series on one afternoon are hours apart. Outside that window the merge is
refused with both timestamps named, and the completeness gate never sees a foreign row.
A regression test reproduces tonight exactly: 11:14 against 17:00, same uid, same day,
now refused.
